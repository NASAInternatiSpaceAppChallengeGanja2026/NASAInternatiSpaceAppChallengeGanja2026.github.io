Greenhouse Supplement Modeling Notes (Synthetic Scenario)

How fresh crop buffers interact with stored rations in our prototype

1. Fresh Vitamin C & B1 Buffer

Prepackaged rations lose heat-sensitive vitamins over multi-year storage (Cooper et al. 2017; Goulette et al. 2020). Our synthetic greenhouse module models how small hydroponic beds (microgreens, tomatoes, sweet potatoes, spirulina) can supplement daily micronutrient intake.

2. Illustrative Parameters

Yield rates, oxygen output, and nutrient densities in crop_models.json are illustrative synthetic parameters inspired by NASA BVAD biomass production discussions, not physical greenhouse measurements from this hackathon.

3. Coupled Actionable Guidance

When the Crew Health forecast detects a drop in stored vitamin C or thiamine B1 (such as after a cooling failure), the decision engine recommends specific servings from the available fresh crop and ration inventory.

1. Arrhenius Kinetic Law

Reaction rate constant k as a function of absolute temperature T:

k = A · exp( -E_a / (R · T) )

Where A is the pre-exponential frequency factor, E_a is activation energy (kJ/mol), R = 8.314 J/(mol·K), and T is storage temperature in Kelvin.

2. Constrained Linear Programming

Minimize objective cost (payload stowage mass or chilling energy):

min cᵀx subject to A_ub · x ≤ b_ub, x ≥ 0

Subject to daily bounds: Thiamine ≥ 1.2 mg & Ascorbic acid ≥ 90 mg (NASA JSC 2005); Energy ≥ 2,800 kcal, Protein ≥ 85 g, Water ≤ 2.5 L/day (assumed).

3. Synthetic Crop Yield Scaling

Daily biomass yield under controlled environment (synthetic model):

Y_biomass = Area · β_crop · √( DLI_actual / DLI_nominal )

Where DLI = (PPFD · Photoperiod · 3600) / 10⁶. Uses illustrative crop entries from crop_models.json (microgreens, spirulina, tomatoes, sweet potatoes).

4. Serum 25(OH)D Decay

First-order serum vitamin D trajectory without UV-B exposure:

D(t+1) = D_t(1 - k_D) + α_D · I_{D,t}

Uses k_D = 0.00181 day⁻¹ (~25% loss over 180 days without supplementation; Rice et al., NTRS 20080026180) and insufficiency threshold 50 nmol/L.

5. Crew Vitamin Pool & Sols-to-Risk

First-order physiological vitamin balance and analytic time-to-risk:

P(t+1) = min(P_max, P_t(1-k) + a·I_t) • t* = ln((P_0-P_eq)/(P_thr-P_eq))/k

Couples Arrhenius ration retention into astronaut body pools (Vit C k = 0.0225/Sol, B1 k = 0.0385/Sol) with personal baseline ±2σ z-scores.

6. ECLSS Sabatier & OGS Loop

Electrolytic oxygen generation and catalytic Sabatier water recovery:

CO₂ + 4H₂ → CH₄ + 2H₂O (BVAD Stoichiometry)

Stoichiometric OGS (1.126 kg H₂O/kg O₂) and Sabatier (0.818 kg H₂O/kg CO₂) with NASA BVAD crew rates (0.816 kg O₂, 1.04 kg CO₂/CM-d) and assumed water loop recovery.

03 • Limitations & Next Steps Honest Audit
What Doesn't Work Yet (Limitations):
  • Synthetic crew and scenarios: No human has flown a 450-Sol mission, so the crew trajectories are synthetic. The real Inspiration4 data has 4 people, 3 baseline tests and a 3-day mission.
  • Single flags are mostly noise: 26% flagged before flight (L-3) vs 20% at R+1.
  • Literature check: only 7 of 12 published retention endpoints agree with our synthetic catalog (RMSE about 27 percentage points).
  • Simulated sensors: soil moisture sensors are software-emulated, not hardware.
If We Had More Time (Roadmap):
  • Hardware-in-the-Loop: Test real moisture sensors on a small crop bed.
  • Radiation + Thermal Kinetics: Couple GCR ionizing dose into Arrhenius decay k(T, D_rad).
  • Live Wearable Stream: Connect real-time smartwatch HRV & sleep telemetry into the anomaly engine.
